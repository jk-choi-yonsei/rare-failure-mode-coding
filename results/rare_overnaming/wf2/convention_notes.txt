1. When Philips CPAP/BiPAP reports cite "recall notification related to the sound abatement foam" or alleged illness with "no allegation of serious or permanent harm", they are filed under "Material Integrity Problem > Degraded", not "No Apparent Adverse Event > Prophylactic removal due to corrective action".
2. When a pacemaker was "pacing below the lower rate" (or programmed rate) with bradycardia, it is filed under "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Pacing Problem", not "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Pacing Problem > Pacing Inadequately" (kept for pacing stopping during ablation).
3. When a glucose sensor report says "early sensor expiration" occurred (or an early sensor replacement alert), it is filed under "Protective Measures Problem > Premature Indicator Activation > Premature End-of-Life Indicator", not "Protective Measures Problem > Premature Indicator Activation".
4. When a lead or pacemaker was "explanted due to an unknown product performance issue", it is filed under "Manufacturing, Packaging or Shipping Problem > Defective Device", not "No Apparent Adverse Event > Prophylactic removal due to corrective action" (kept for devices "functioning as intended").
5. When insulin infusion set "tubing detached from the connector" or hub, it is filed under "Mechanical Problem > Detachment of Device or Device Component", not "Connection Problem > Decoupling", which is kept for needles coming off syringes and endotracheal tube ports coming apart.
6. When a spinal cord stimulator report says the patient had "inadequate stimulation" leading to explant, revision or replacement, it is filed under "Output Problem > Unexpected Therapeutic Results", not "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Pacing Problem > Pacing Inadequately".
7. When an insulin pump "malfunction alarm" or code was "not resettable", it is filed under "Infusion or Flow Problem > Pumping Problem > Pumping Stopped", not "Protective Measures Problem > Reset Problem > Failure to Reset" (kept for pumps not restarting after occlusion).
8. When a clear-aligner report says the dentist or orthodontist advised the patient to "cease" or stop treatment, it is filed under "Patient Device Interaction Problem > Patient-Device Incompatibility", not "No Apparent Adverse Event > Prophylactic removal due to corrective action".
9. When a pacemaker reports "code 1003" or "high impedance within the battery", it is filed under "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Battery Problem > Premature Discharge of Battery", not "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Battery Problem > Battery Problem: High Impedance".
10. When a neurostimulator lead gave "ineffective stimulation" leading to surgery, with no impedance, migration or fracture finding, it is filed under "Output Problem > Energy Output Problem > Therapeutic or Diagnostic Output Failure", not "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Pacing Problem > Pacing Inadequately".
11. When a transcatheter valve report gives only a patient event (pacemaker implanted "on the same day as the implant", hospitalization), no valve malfunction, it is filed under "Patient Device Interaction Problem", not "No Apparent Adverse Event > Prophylactic removal due to corrective action".
12. When an implanted intrathecal drug pump report involves the patient having an "MRI", it is most often filed under "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Electromagnetic Compatibility Problem > Electromagnetic Interference", not "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Pacing Problem > Pacing Inadequately" or another pacing label.
13. When an endoscope "tested positive" for colony forming units, bacteria or biofilm, it is filed under "Contamination /Decontamination Problem > Contamination > Microbial Contamination of Device", not "Contamination /Decontamination Problem > Device Reprocessing Problem > Failure to Disinfect" (kept for disinfection not done).
14. When an insulin pump report says "cartridge change errors occurred" during the load sequence, it is filed under "Mechanical Problem", not "Computer Software Problem > Application Program Problem > Application Program Problem: Medication Error".
15. When "a right heart catheterization was performed to confirm the validity of the pressure sensor readings", it is filed under "Output Problem > Incorrect, Inadequate or Imprecise Result or Readings > Incorrect Measurement", not "No Apparent Adverse Event > Prophylactic removal due to corrective action".
16. When an insulin pump report says "an o-ring was on the pneumatic tap", it is filed under "Mechanical Problem > Detachment of Device or Device Component", not "Manufacturing, Packaging or Shipping Problem > Device Misassembled During Manufacturing /Shipping > Extra Components".
17. When an endoscope shows a foreign object "during the device evaluation", it is filed under "Contamination /Decontamination Problem > Device Reprocessing Problem > Failure to Clean Adequately", not "Manufacturing, Packaging or Shipping Problem > Device Misassembled During Manufacturing /Shipping > Extra Components".
18. When no "drops of insulin" appear at the tubing end during the fill tubing step, it is filed under "Infusion or Flow Problem > Priming Problem" (in 2024 Filling Problem), not "Infusion or Flow Problem > Priming Problem > Incomplete or Inadequate Priming".
19. When only a patient reaction is reported, such as skin irritation under a "LifeVest" or "pericardial effusion that required pericardiocentesis" after ablation, it is filed under "Patient Device Interaction Problem", not "No Apparent Adverse Event > Prophylactic removal due to corrective action".
20. When an insulin pump report says the patient received an "uncommanded bolus", it is filed under "Mechanical Problem > Unintended Movement > Unintended System Motion", not "Computer Software Problem > Application Program Problem > Application Program Problem: Medication Error".
21. When an insulin pump report says the customer experienced a "motor error alarm", it is filed under "Mechanical Problem", not "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Pacing Problem > Pacing Inadequately".
22. When a neurostimulator charging report mentions the system message "RM03", it is filed under "Temperature Problem > Overheating of Device", not "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Charging Problem > Aborted Charge".
23. When a continuous glucose monitor report says "signal loss" occurred, it is filed under "Communication or Transmission Problem > Wireless Communication Problem", not "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Device Sensing Problem > Loss of Threshold".
24. When an x-ray or angiography system report says "disk full", "low storage" or insufficient disk space, it is filed under "Output Problem > Radiation Output Problem > Radiation Output Failure", not "Computer Software Problem > Data Back-Up Problem > Failure to Back-Up".
25. When a defibrillator displays a "defib charging error" message, it is filed under "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Charging Problem > Failure to Charge", not "Electrical /Electronic Property Problem > Charging Problem > Aborted Charge".
